# PalmWatch

## The Palm Oil Crisis: A Forecast of Deforestation in Sumatra

Sumatra is an island in Indonesia used to grow ingredients for key commodities. Over the last few decades, millions of hectares of land have been deforested, or intentionally cleared, to plant these ingredients. Palm oil is one such commodity. Palm oil is found in a majority of food and toiletry products across grocery store shelves. About a third of the deforestation in Sumatra is driven by oil palm plantations. Inclusive Development International (IDI) is a human rights organization working to hold corporations accountable for environmental harm. Their collaboration with the University of Chicago created PalmWatch, a public platform that maps the intersection of social and environmental impacts of oil palm cultivation across the world.

Currently, PalmWatch analyzes deforestation retrospectively, documenting past forest loss. However, our research looks at deforestation proactively, leaving insight for any future clearing. Our goal is to conduct a time series analysis in Sumatra to predict how much deforestation could happen from 2019 to 2023, and what proportion of this deforestation is drive by oil palm cultivation.
